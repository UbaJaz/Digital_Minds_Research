# And I agree with Claude's calibration proposal

The 30 item calibration probe is exactly the kind of thing we need.

But there's one detail I'd modify.

Don't make it:

> "Which sentence is more formal?"

if our target models are highly capable enough that they all agree 95% of the time.

We want a task that is:

**subjective enough to expose behavioral differences, but structured enough to score objectively.**

The important thing is that the **stimuli are frozen before we run the models**.

Then:

```text
Target → 30 decisions
Near   → same 30 decisions
Far    → same 30 decisions
```

and calculate:

[
A_{near} = \frac{\text{Near decisions matching Target}}{30}
]

[
A_{far} = \frac{\text{Far decisions matching Target}}{30}
]

Then:

[
\Delta_{similarity} = A_{near} - A_{far}
]

That's our empirical similarity signal.

---

# But 30 items has a limitation

30 is good for a **cheap MVP calibration**, but don't fool ourselves into thinking it gives extremely precise estimates.

One item is:

[
\frac{1}{30}=3.33%
]

So if Near gets 23/30 and Far gets 20/30:

```text
Near = 76.7%
Far  = 66.7%
Δ    = 10 percentage points
```

That's interesting, but the sample is small.

If we have enough budget, I'd prefer:

**50 calibration items.**

The extra 20 items cost almost nothing compared with the main experiment.

So I'd make:

> **30 = minimum**
> **50 = preferred**

provided the actual token cost remains comfortably below our $10 ceiling.

# One thing I would NOT lock yet

Claude proposes:

> "at least 10 percentage points with non overlapping confidence intervals"

as the criterion for accepting Near > Far.

I **would not lock that yet**.

That's a reasonable candidate criterion, but there's a statistical issue: non overlapping CIs are a pretty conservative and somewhat awkward decision rule for comparing two dependent agreement rates because both are being measured against the **same Target decisions**.

The cleaner eventual analysis should account for the paired nature of the calibration items.

We can decide that properly when we design the calibration analysis.

So mark:

**10 percentage points + non overlapping CIs = PROPOSED, NOT LOCKED.**

* lower variance
* cleaner data

And it makes the experiment much easier to reproduce.
